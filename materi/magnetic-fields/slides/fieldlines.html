§ 24.1 · Representing a field

Four rules for field lines

1

Direction

Where the N end of a compass points. Outside a magnet N to S, inside S to N.

2

Spacing

Closer lines mean a stronger field, a larger B.

3

Never cross

B has one direction at each point, so lines cannot meet.

4

Closed loops

No start and no end. There is no isolated N or S pole.

Follow the moving compass in the animation: it always points along the line through its centre, so a field line is simply the path a compass would trace. Rule 4 is the deep one. Compare with electric field lines, which start on positive and end on negative charges. Magnetic field lines never end because there is no magnetic charge.
