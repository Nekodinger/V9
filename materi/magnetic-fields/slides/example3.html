Worked example 3

Force between wires, and a zero-field point

(a) FORCE

(b) ZERO FIELD

Two long parallel wires 4.0 cm apart carry 5.0 A and 2.0 A in the same direction. Find the force on 0.50 m of each wire.

Two parallel wires 8.0 cm apart carry 6.0 A and 2.0 A in the same direction. Where between them is B = 0?

B₁ = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 5.0) ÷ (2π × 0.040)
B₁ = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ T

F = B₁I₂L = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 2.0 × 0.50
F = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ N, attractive, on each wire

Between like currents the two fields point in opposite directions, so they can cancel there. Let x be the distance from the 6.0 A wire.

μ₀(6.0) ÷ 2πx = μ₀(2.0) ÷ 2π(0.080 − x)
6.0(0.080 − x) = 2.0x ⟹ 0.48 = 8.0x

x = 0.060 m, 6.0 cm from the 6.0 A wire

For (a), μ0 / 2π = 2 × 10⁻⁷ makes the arithmetic quick: B1 = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 5.0 / 0.040. For (b), the μ0 and 2π cancel, so the condition is simply I1 / x = I2 / (d − x). Ask: if the currents were in opposite directions, where would the zero-field point be? Outside the pair, on the side of the smaller current: 6/x = 2/(x − 0.08) gives x = 0.12 m, that is 4.0 cm beyond the 2.0 A wire.
