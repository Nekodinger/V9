§ 20.4 · How strong is the field?

Near a wire, and inside a solenoid

LONG STRAIGHT WIRE

B =

μ₀I

2πr

r is the perpendicular distance from the wire. Double the distance, halve the field.

I = 10 A, r = 5.0 cm: B = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 10) ÷ (2π × 0.050) = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ T, close to the Earth’s field.

LONG SOLENOID

B = μ₀nI

n = N / l, turns per metre

Inside, away from the ends, B does not depend on the radius of the solenoid or the position.

A ferrous core multiplies B many times: the iron becomes magnetised and adds its own field.

μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H m⁻¹ (permeability of free space)

These two results come from Ampère’s law, which is beyond A Level; students use them, not derive them. The 1/r dependence matches the spreading rings in the Meeting 1 animation.
