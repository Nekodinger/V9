§ 20.2 · Measuring B: the analysis

From a balance reading to B

01

Extra downward force on the magnet (Δm in kg)

Fmagnet = Δm g

02

Newton’s third law: an equal and opposite force on the wire

Fwire = Δm g

03

Wire perpendicular to B, so use F = BIL

Δm g = BIL

04

Rearrange for B

B =

Δm g

IL

05

Better: plot Δm g against I. Gradient = BL, so B = gradient ÷ L.

RELIABLE RESULTS

Length L is the wire inside the field, about the width of the pole faces; the field weakens at the edges.

Heating: use small currents for short times so readings do not drift.

Offsets: reverse the current and average the two readings.

Alternative: a calibrated Hall probe (§ 20.3).

Step 2 is the conceptual heart: the balance measures the force on the magnet, not on the wire. Students often forget to convert grams to kilograms in step 1. The graph method in step 5 uses all the data and reduces the effect of random error in single readings; a non-zero intercept would show a systematic error such as a zero error on the balance.
