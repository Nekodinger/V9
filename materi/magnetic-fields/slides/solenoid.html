§ 24.1 · Fields due to currents

A flat coil and a long solenoid

Inside a long solenoid B is uniform and along the axis; outside it resembles a bar magnet. A ferrous (iron) core makes B much stronger.

Both diagrams are cross-sections: each turn of wire cuts the page twice, once with current coming out (dot) and once going in (cross). Apply the grip rule to each wire and the fields add up inside. Pole rule: look at one end of the solenoid; if the current flows anticlockwise that end is N, clockwise is S. The iron core works because its domains line up with the solenoid field and add their own field, typically hundreds of times larger. Real solenoids are 3D, so the outside field spreads more than this 2D model shows.
