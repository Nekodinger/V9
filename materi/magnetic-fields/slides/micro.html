Where F = BIL comes from

Fig. 3 Length L, area A, n carriers per m³, each of charge q, drift speed v. Field B into the page.

The force on the wire is the sum of the magnetic forces on its moving charges, passed to the metal lattice through collisions.

01

Current in terms of the carriers

I = nAvq

02

Number of carriers in length L

N = nAL

03

Force on one moving charge, v ⊥ B

f = Bqv

04

Add the forces on all N carriers

F = Nf = (nAL)(Bqv)

05

Regroup to recognise I

F = B(nAvq)L = BIL

This derivation connects Meeting 2 with topic 20.3 (charged particles in fields), where F = BQv sin θ is studied in its own right. Students met I = nAvq in the current electricity topic. The result shows why the force on a wire is the same whether the carriers are positive charges moving one way or electrons moving the other way: q and v both change sign, so qv, and therefore the force, is unchanged.
