§ 20.2 · Measuring B with a current balance

Weighing a magnetic force

StepProcedure
1Stand a U-shaped magnet on a top-pan balance; zero the reading.
2Clamp a stiff wire between the poles, at 90° to B, not touching the magnet.
3Pass a current I and record the new reading.
4Wire pushed up, so the magnet is pushed down (Newton’s third law): the reading rises by Δm.
5Repeat for several currents, and with the current reversed.

The animation uses B = 0.060 T and L = 5.0 cm, so each extra ampere adds 3.0 mN, about 0.31 g, to the reading. The wire is seen end-on with the current coming out of the page; the field points from N to S, so by the left-hand rule the force on the wire is upwards. This is the same Current Balance practical used in LKPD 2. If the reading falls instead of rising, the force on the wire is downwards: reverse the current or turn the magnet round.
