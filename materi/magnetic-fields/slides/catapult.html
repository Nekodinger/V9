§ 20.2 · The motor effect

Why the wire moves

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, not parallel to it, experiences a force.

  1. The magnet gives a uniform field.
  2. The current gives circular field lines round the wire.
  3. The fields add on one side and partly cancel on the other.
  4. The wire is pushed from the strong side to the weak side.

The force is at right angles to both the current and the field.
