B from current balance data

A wire with 5.0 cm inside the field is used in a current balance. Determine B and the direction of the force on the wire.

01 · Δm is proportional to I, so the force is too

gradient of Δm against I = 1.22 g ÷ 4.0 A = 0.305 g A⁻¹

02 · Convert to force per ampere: g to kg, then × g

F/I = 0.305 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 = 2.99 × 10⁻³ N A⁻¹ = BL

03 · Divide by L = 0.050 m

B = 2.99 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.050 = 0.060 T

04 · Direction

The reading rises, so the magnet is pushed down. By Newton’s third law the force on the wire is upwards.

A least-squares line through the origin gives the same gradient, 3.00 mN per ampere, so B = 0.060 T to 2 s.f. Extension: if L is 5.0 ± 0.2 cm and the gradient is ± 2%, the percentage uncertainty in B is 4% + 2% = 6%, so B = 0.060 ± 0.004 T. These are the same values used in the animation on slide 18.
